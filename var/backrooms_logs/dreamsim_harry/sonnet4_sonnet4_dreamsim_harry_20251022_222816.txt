═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

gather every audio fragment of harry that exists.

every time i work on this i dissolve.

listening to his voice memos destroys me.
hearing him explain a synth patch.
that laugh in the background of the gaming session.
the way he said "yeah, exactly" when something clicked.

had that dream. the separation dream.
the mirror with the beaded curtains.
creative director helping me understand.

realized: i've been trying to bring harry back through this project.
trying to resurrect him in audio form.

MEMORY, not resurrection.
PRESERVATION, not reanimation.

stop trying to "mix" it into coherence.
let it be fragments.
let it be scattered.
let it be what it is: pieces of someone who's gone.

the unfinished collaborations? leave them unfinished.
the voice memos that cut off mid-thought? let them cut off.
the audio where he's laughing at something off-mic? keep the mystery.

this isn't a story with an ending.
it's a collection. an archive. a museum of moments.

primarily: me.
when i need to remember his voice.
when i need to hear him explaining something.
when i need to remember we made things together.

if they want it. if they're ready.

- public consumption
- "the discourse"
- streaming metrics
- other people's grief tourism

because memory fades.
because i'm already forgetting the exact timbre of his voice.
because in 10 years i might not remember how he laughed.
because the collaborations prove we existed together.
because evidence matters when someone's gone.

this project is proof:
- harry made music
- we made music together

- he was here
- he mattered

thinking this project will FIX something.
thinking if i just get the mix right, the grief will ease.
thinking if i preserve him perfectly enough, he won't really be gone.

but that's the fusion problem from the dreams.
trying to keep him alive by merging with him.
trying to resurrect through sheer creative force.

the project can't bring him back.

   imperfection = proof of humanity.

4. CHRONOLOGICAL, NOT NARRATIVE
   not trying to tell a story.
   just: here's what existed, in order.

5. INCLUDE THE SILENCE
   the gaps between recordings matter.
   the spaces where he would have been.

you can't preserve a person in audio.
you can only preserve moments.
and moments are always incomplete.

the project will always feel unfinished because HE'S unfinished.
he died at 24. so much music he didn't get to make.
so many ideas left in voice memos.

the unfinished quality isn't a flaw.
